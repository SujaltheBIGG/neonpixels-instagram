Why the grid stays intact

  1. Instagram adds every new post to the top left of the grid and pushes all the others along by one tile.
  2. The 15 launch posts make 5 full rows of 3. Each row was designed as a set: the project post always sits on the right, and the rows alternate between light and dark.
  3. Three new posts add exactly one full row on top, so every launch row stays as designed.
  4. Putting all five in the grid would push every row along by two tiles. Each designed row would be split over two lines, and the project posts would leave the right-hand column (see the comparison below).
  5. So posts 19 and 20 are reels published without a grid tile. They go live on the account, appear in the profile’s Reels tab and can be shown in the Reels feed like any reel. They just don’t take a place in the grid.
  6. The new row follows the same logic: 16, a project, on the right; 17, a review, in the middle, like 08 and 14; 18, the method, on the left, like 12. It reads dark · light · dark, alternating with the row below it.
  7. After that, three posts a week add one full row each week, so the grid stays aligned from then on.
